The method

Playing shouldn’t
be a struggle.

If you open the case every day hoping the sound will finally match the ear, you don’t need more hours — you need the right sequence. The studio builds three things, in this order.

  1. 01
    Scales & techniqueFrom the beginning, scales of every kind sit at the root of everything that follows: all keys, every speed, many bowings, and double stops that stretch the hand’s real potential.
  2. 02
    Études & musicianshipKreutzer, Rode and ultimately the Paganini Caprices — taught alongside ear training, pitch discernment, rhythm reading and textural awareness, because technique without the ear is only gymnastics.
  3. 03
    Practising with a purposeThe critical skill is knowing what to do when you go home. The aim is measurable progress, big or small, every single day — real change rather than repetition.
  4. 04
    Performing under pressureNo one is immune to nerves. The mental game of violin is the mental game of a speech or a final quarter — so it is rehearsed, not wished away.

Richard Amoroso · performances

A relationship
that began at 14.

Richard’s relationship with The Philadelphia Orchestra began in 1984, at the age of 14, when he won the Orchestra’s Student Competition — now the Albert M. Greenfield Competition — and subsequently performed as a soloist with the Orchestra.

He joined the Orchestra in 1998, after serving with Concerto Soloists (now the Chamber Orchestra of Philadelphia), Peter Nero and the Philly Pops, and as a substitute violinist with the Orchestra — a member of the First Violin Section ever since.
